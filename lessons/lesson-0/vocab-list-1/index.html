{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite greeting used in the morning, such as with superiors or strangers.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} The casual form of おはようございます. Often used with friends or family.{TAGS}
{WORD}*{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A common greeting used in the afternoon.
*The final はha is pronounced "wa", like わ. It is pronounced this way, because こんにちは is a combination of the word こんにちtoday and the topic particle はwa which is pronounced "wa" instead of "ha."{TAGS}
{WORD}*{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A common greeting used in the evening.
*Much like こんにちは, the final は is pronounced "wa", because it is a combination of the word こんばんtonight and the topic particle は.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite way to say goodbye. Often used when you will not see someone for a long time.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A casual way to say goodbye. Often used when you will see someone again soon.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} Similar to またね above, however, if you plan on seeing someone tomorrow, またあした can be used instead. Literally, またね means "again" and またあした means "again tomorrow."{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A highly polite expression used when entering a room or when saying goodbye. Often used when entering a teacher's/boss' office or excusing oneself from a teacher's/boss' office.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite way to say good night.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} The casual form of おやすみなさい.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} An expression used when introducing yourself to someone for the first time. Literally, it is something along the lines of "It's the first time (that I am meeting you)."{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} An expression used at the end of an introduction to express your desire to get along well with the person or people you're introducing yourself to. It is a combination of the adverb form of the formal adjective よろしいyoroshii (good) and おねがいしますonegai shimasu (please do for me...), giving it the literal meaning of "Please be good to me."{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} The casual form of よろしくおねがいします. Not typically used in formal introductions, but may be used among friends when wishing to continue to get along well, such as in the new year.
これから も よろしくkore kara mo yoroshiku！
I hope we can continue to get along!
(lit., "Please be good to me in the future as well.")
{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} An expression typically used when leaving, especially one's own home. Literally, it means "I will go and come back." It is a combination of the verbs いくiku (to go), in the te-form for connecting verbs like "and", and くるkuru (to come), in the polite masu-form.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} An expression used in response to いってきます when seeing someone off. Literally means "Please go and come back." Much like いってきます, it is a combination of いくiku (to go) and らっしゃい, the clipped imperative form of いらっしゃるirassharu (to come/go), which is a very formal variant of the verb くるkuru (to come) in this case.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} An expression used upon return, especially to one's own home. It is short for ただいまかえりましたtadaima kaerimashita (lit., "I have just returned.") which is far more formal and not as commonly used. {TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite expression used to welcome someone back. It is most commonly used after someone has announced their return with ただいま, but it can also be used before someone has announced their return.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} The casual form of おかえりなさい.{TAGS}


Everyday Expressions日常表現にちじょうひょうげん

Practice:練習： Everyday Expressions 1 Everyday Expressions 2
Word言葉 Meaning意味 Notes/Infoノート・情報
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} An expression used before eating a meal to show respect for the food that you're about to receive. It is derived from the humble verb いただくitadaku (to receive), so the literal meaning of this expression is "I (humbly) receive (this meal)."{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite expression used after eating a meal to once again show respect for the food that you have received. It is a combination of many different elements:
  • ごgo - prefix for words to show respect
  • ちそうchisou - "feast"
  • さまsama - suffix to make a word more polite
  • でしたdeshita - "was", past polite form of ですdesu
Simplifying things to ごちそうさま (feast) and でした (was), gives us the literal meaning "It was a feast." {TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} The casual form of ごちそうさまでした.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A very polite expression used to ask a favor of another person. Commonly used when ordering at a restaurant, when asking someone to do something, etc.
みず (を) おねがいしますmizu (wo) onegai shimasu。(を is optional)
Water, please.
{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} どうぞ can be used when:
  • Handing someone something, such as a present.
  • Giving permission, such as entering a room.
どうぞ、はいってくださいdouzo, haitte kudasai。
Please feel free to come on in.
{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} です is a polite sentence-ending copula that can be used for making statements. One common and simple use case is stating your name.
はじめまして、カンたんですhajimemashite, kantan desu！
Nice to meet you, I'm Kan-tan!
{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite word for saying "yes." It can also be used to say "OK" or "I understand."
 Please be careful not to say はい consecutively (e.g., "はい、はい"), however, as it may come off rude or dismissive depending on the context, like "Yeah, whatever..." or "I know, I heard you the first time."{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite word for saying "no." It can also be used as a response for when someone thanks you for something you did. In such a situation, you can modestly reply with "いいえ、いいえ" which roughly translates to "don't mention it" or "it was no trouble."{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite expression used when thanking someone.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} The casual form of ありがとうございます.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite expression used when congratulating someone for something or wishing them well. When used with おたんじょうびotanjoubi (birthday), you can wish someone a happy birthday.
おたんじょうびおめでとうございますotanjoubi omedetou gozaimasu！
Happy Birthday!
{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} The casual form of おめでとうございます.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite way to say "you're welcome" to someone who thanked you.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} A polite expression used when apologizing or excusing oneself. In some cases, it can even be used to thank someone who helped you, which is more akin to saying "Sorry for troubling you." in English.{TAGS}
{WORD}{BUTTONS} {MEANING} An expression used when apologizing. It can be shortened to just ごめん, in very casual situations.{TAGS}
