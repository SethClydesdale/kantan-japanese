Hello Words

The greeting that everyone learns first is to say hello and you may already know a few of these! Japanese has 3 different words for conveying this with each one being used at a different time of day.

Word こんにちはkonnichiwa
Meaning hello; good afternoon
Usage Often used as a greeting between noon and evening.

The first and most widely known greeting is こんにちはkonnichiwa which means "hello" or "good afternoon." This greeting is mainly used between noon to evening hours.

カンたんkan-tan
セス、こんにちは！sesu konnichiwa!
セスsesu
あ、カンたん！こんにちは。a, kan-tan! konnichiwa.
Kan-tan
Hi, Seth!
Seth
Oh, Kan-tan! Hello.

You might be wondering, why is the ending pronounced as わwa when the last Hiragana is はha? The reason for this is that こんにちは is a combination of the word こんにちkonnichi (today) and the topic particle はwa which is pronounced like わwa instead of "ha." This combination roughly translates to "Today is..." but feeling and usage-wise is closer to the more simple "hello."

We'll learn about the topic particle はwa later on, but to give you a general idea now, it's basically used to mark something as the topic of a sentence/conversation.


Word おはよう(ございます)ohayou(gozaimasu)
Meaning good morning
Usage Often used as a greeting between morning and noon.
おはよう = casual (used with friends, family, subordinates)
おはようございます = polite (used with people you don't know, superiors)

Another word for saying hello is おはよう(ございます)ohayou(gozaimasu) which means "good morning." This greeting is mainly used between morning to noon hours.

Unlike こんにちはkonnichiwa, however, おはようohayou has two forms: a casual form (おはようohayou) and a polite form (おはようございますohayou gozaimasu). The casual form is used with friends, family, and subordinates, whereas the polite form is used with people you don't know and superiors.

カンたんkan-tan
せんせい、おはようございます！sensei, ohayou gozaimasu!
せんせいsensei
おはよう。カンたんはあいかわらずげんきね。ohayou. kan-tan wa aikawarazu genki ne.
Kan-tan
Good morning, teacher!
Teacher
Good morning. You're as energetic as ever, Kan-tan.
In Japanese, it is very common to switch between using polite forms (which are often longer) and casual forms (which are often shorter) depending on who you're talking to. When speaking to superiors, such as your boss, teacher, etc., the polite form is often used. When speaking to friends, family, or equals, however, those niceties are often dropped in lieu of more casual forms to convey that you're on closer terms.

Word こんばんはkonbanwa
Meaning good evening
Usage Often used as a greeting between evening and morning.

Last up for the hello words is こんばんはkonbanwa which means "good evening." This greeting is mainly used between evening to morning hours.

カンたんkan-tan
ねこちゃん、こんばんは！neko-chan, konbanwa!
ねこneko
にゃあ～。nyaa.
Kan-tan
Good evening, little kitty!
Cat
Meow~.

Just like with こんにちはkonnichiwa, the final はha in こんばんはkonbanwa is pronounced like わwa because it's a combination of こんばんkonban (tonight) and the topic particle はwa.

Goodbye Words

Now that you've learned how to say hello in Japanese, the next step is to learn how to say goodbye!

Word さようならsayounara
Meaning farewell; goodbye
Usage A polite way to say goodbye.
Often used when you will not see someone again for a long time.

さようならsayounara is a word used to bid farewell to someone who you may not see again for a long time.

カンたんのおとうさんkan-tan no otou-san
いちねんかんアメリカにいく。さようなら、カンたん。ichinenkan amerika ni iku. sayounara kan-tan.
カンたんkan-tan
ぐすっ、さようなら、おとうさん…gusu, sayounara, otou-san...
Kan-tan's Father
I'll be going to America for a year. Goodbye, Kan-tan.
Kan-tan
*sniff* Goodbye, Dad...
ぐすっgusu is a shortened form of ぐすぐすgusu gusu, an onomatopoeiamimetic word often used for sad sniffling.

Because さようならsayounara carries a sense of finality with it, it is mainly used only when you don't expect to see someone again for a very long time.

Word またあしたmata ashita / またねmata ne
Meaning see you tomorrow/later; bye
Usage A casual way to say goodbye.
Often used when you will see someone again tomorrow/soon.

またあしたmata ashita / またねmata ne is a casual way to say goodbye to someone who you will see again soon. Unlike with さようならsayounara it doesn't carry that sense of finality with it as its literal meaning is "again tomorrow" and the further shortened form simply meaning "again." In English, these are akin to saying "See you tomorrow" and "See you later."

カンたんkan-tan
きょうはたのしかった！またね！kyou wa tanoshikatta! mata ne!
ともだちtomodachi
うん、またあした！un, mata ashita!
Kan-tan
I had fun today! See you later!
Friend
Yep, I'll see you tomorrow!

Aside from また(あした)mata (ashita), there is also the more casual じゃあねjaa ne (see ya) or the more polite ではまたdewa mata (see you later) which can also be contracted to the more casual じゃまたja mata.

Word しつれいしますshitsurei shimasu
Meaning excuse me; pardon me
Usage A highly polite expression used when entering a room or leaving.
Often used when entering a teacher's/boss' office or excusing oneself from a teacher's/boss' office.

しつれいしますshitsurei shimasu is a formal expression which translates to "excuse me." It can be used in any formal situation that requires respectful speech, such as when entering or leaving a teacher's office.

カンたんkan-tan
しつれいします。ちょっといいですか？shitsurei shimasu. chotto ii desu ka?
せんせいsensei
はい、どうぞ。hai, douzo.
Kan-tan
Excuse me. Is now a good time (to ask you a question)?
Teacher
Sure, please come in.

Word おやすみ(なさい)oyasumi(nasai)
Meaning good night
Usage An expression used when saying goodnight.
おやすみ = casual
おやすみなさい = more polite

When saying good night to someone you can use the expression おやすみなさいoyasumi nasai.

カンたんkan-tan
おかあさん、おやすみ～！okaa-san, oyasumi~!
カンたんのおかあさんkan-tan no okaa-san
おやすみなさい。oyasumi nasai
Kan-tan
Good night, mom!
Kan-tan's Mother
Good night, dear.

If the なさいnasai ending is dropped, it makes this expression more casual.

Introductory Words

The following words are commonly used together when introducing yourself, and we'll show you how to use them below so that you can introduce yourself to people in Japanese!

Word はじめましてhajimemashite
Meaning nice to meet you; how do you do?
Usage An expression used when introducing yourself to someone for the first time.

Word よろしく(おねがいします)yoroshiku onegai shimasu
Meaning I hope we can get along; please treat me well
Usage An expression used at the end of an introduction to express your desire to get along well with the person or people you're introducing yourself to.
よろしく = casual
よろしくおねがいします = polite

Word ～ですdesu
Meaning I am...; ~ is...
Usage です is a polite sentence-ending copula that can be used for making statements. When introducing oneself, for example, you can say NAMEです to say "I am/My name is...NAME."

As mentioned above, all of these words can be used together in a simple introduction and we'll start you off with the template below. Simply replace NAME with your own, and you can start introducing yourself to people in Japanese!

はじめまして、NAMEです。hajimemashite, NAME desu.
よろしくおねがいします。yoroshiku onegai shimasu
Nice to meet you, I'm NAME.
I hope we can get along.

While はじめまして and です are straightforward in their meanings, よろしくおねがいします is a bit of an enigma to new learners as it can convey multiple different meanings depending on the context. In the context of a first meeting, however, it's more akin to saying "I hope we can get along." or "I look forward to getting acquainted with you."

The above introduction is simplified to help give you an idea of how to introduce yourself in Japanese. After the initial はじめまして statement, you can include some additional information about yourself if you want, such as your occupation, hobbies, and so on to help people to get to know you.

カンたんはクラスのみんなにじこしょうかいをします。kan-tan wa kurasu no minna ni jikoshoukai wo shimasu.
カンたんkan-tan
はじめまして、カンたんです！hajimemashite kan-tan desu!
カンたんkan-tan
ねことおかしがだいすきです！neko to okashi ga daisuki desu!
カンたんkan-tan
よろしくおねがいします！yoroshiku onegai shimasu!
カンたんはおじぎをします。kan-tan wa ojigi wo shimasu.
Kan-tan introduces herself to the class.
Kan-tan
Nice to meet you, I'm Kan-tan!
Kan-tan
I LOVE cats and candy!
Kan-tan
I hope we can all be friends!
Kan-tan bows.

This type of introduction might be a bit difficult right now, but once you learn more words and grammar you'll be able to provide more information about yourself during introductions!

In Japan, it is common practice to bow when greeting others, apologizing, showing gratitude/respect, and so on. This can either be a slight nod or a full bend at the waist. If you want to convey the utmost respect to whoever you're talking to, the longer and deeper the bow, the better!

Leaving and Returning Words

In this section we'll learn about words that are commonly used when one is leaving and returning. You'll often see these used with families when someone leaves or comes back home.

Word いってきますittekimasu
Meaning I'm off; see you later
Usage An expression typically used when leaving, especially one's own home.

Word いってらっしゃいitterasshai
Meaning have a good day; take care; see you
Usage An expression used in response to いってきます when seeing someone off.

いってきますittekimasu and いってらっしゃいitterasshai are often used as a pair. いってきます (lit., "I'll go and come back.") is used by the one who is leaving and いってらっしゃい (lit., "Please go and come back.") is said towards the one who is leaving.

カンたんkan-tan
いってきま～す！ittekima~su!
カンたんのおかあさんkan-tan no okaa-san
いってらっしゃい。itterrashai.
Kan-tan
I'm heading out!
Kan-tan's Mother
Take care, dear.

In the example above, Kan-tan is leaving her home to go out, so she says "いってきます" to let her mother know that she'll be leaving and will come back home later. Kan-tan's mother says "いってらっしゃい" in response to tell her to go and come back. In English, this exchange normally translates to "I'm heading out." and "Take care."


Word ただいまtadaima
Meaning I'm home/back
Usage An expression used upon return, especially to one's own home.

Word おかえり(なさい)okaeri(nasai)
Meaning welcome home/back
Usage Used to welcome someone back. It is most commonly used after someone has announced their return with ただいま, but it can also be used before someone has announced their return.

Similar to the previous set of words, ただいまtadaima and おかえり(なさい)okaeri(nasai) are often used as a pair. ただいま (lit., "I have just returned.") is used by the one who has returned and おかえり(なさい) is said towards the one who has just returned.

カンたんkan-tan
ただいま～！tadaima~!
カンたんのおかあさんkan-tan no okaa-san
おかえり。okaeri.
Kan-tan
I'm baaack!
Kan-tan's Mother
Welcome home, dear.

In the example above, Kan-tan has just returned home and cheerfully yells "ただいま～" to let her mother know that she's back. Kan-tan's mother responds by saying "おかえり" to welcome her daughter back home. In English, this exchange is similar to saying "I'm home." and "Welcome home."

ただいまtadaima is derived from the much longer phrase ただいまかえりましたtadaima kaeri mashita which is far more formal and not as commonly used. The meaning of this full phrase is literally "I have just returned." and this meaning carries over to its abbreviated form. You'll often see this longer form used in formal situations, such as saying that "you're back" to your professor if you had previously said "いってきますittekimasu" when you left.

Meal Time Words

The following words are commonly used before and after meals.

Word いただきますitadakimasu
Meaning thank you for the meal (before eating)
Usage An expression used before eating a meal to show respect/thanks for the food that you're about to receive.

Word ごちそうさま(でした)gochisousama (deshita)
Meaning thank you for the meal (after eating); that was a delicious meal
Usage An expression used after eating a meal to show respect/thanks for the food that you have received.

These two words are used as a pair during meals. いただきますitadakimasu is said before a meal to give thanks for the food, and ごちそうさま(でした)gochisousama (deshita) on the other hand is said after a meal to once again give thanks for the food that you have eaten. When saying these words, it is common to take both hands and place them together in front of you as if you're praying.

ばんごはんのまえbangohan no mae
カンたんkan-tan
いただきま～す！itadakima~su!
ばんごはんのあとbangohan no ato
カンたんkan-tan
ごちそうさまでした！gochisousama deshita!
Before dinner
Kan-tan
Thank you for the food!
After dinner
Kan-tan
That was yummy!

In the example above, Kan-tan sits down for dinner, places both of her hands together and says "いただきま～す" to give thanks for the food. After she has finished eating, she once again puts both of her hands together and says "ごちそうさまでした" to give thanks one last time for the tasty food that she has eaten.

To try and draw an example some may be familiar with, the closest custom to this in Western society is "saying grace" before a meal which is common among Christians and other faiths. This often consists of a prayer said before a meal to give thanks to their lord for the food. The only difference with this example is that grace is not said as often after a meal as ごちそうさまでした is in Japanese. That, and いただきます/ごちそうさま have no religious connotations like grace does.

Thanking and Congratulatory Words

Do you want to thank or congratulate someone in Japanese? Then you'll want to add the words below to your vocabulary!

Word ありがとう(ございます)arigatou (gozaimasu)
Meaning thank you; thanks
Usage An expression used when thanking someone.
ありがとう = casual
ありがとうごいざいます = polite

ありがとう(ございます)arigatou (gozaimasu) is a common way to thank someone. ありがとうございます is the more polite form and ありがとう is the more casual form.

がくせいgakusei
はー…テストにおちた…haa... tesuto ni ochita...
カンたんkan-tan
どうぞ、キャンディです！douzo, kyandi desu!
がくせいgakusei
あっ！ありがとう！a! arigatou!
Student
*sigh* I failed the test...
Kan-tan
Here, have some candy!
Student
Oh! Thanks!

To thank someone for something specifically, use the following pattern: FAVORをありがとう(ございます).

キャンディをありがとうございます！kyandi wo arigatou gozaimasu!
Thank you for the candy!

This example uses the direct object particle をwo (pronounced おo), which is something we'll learn about later on. In short, it connects the FAVOR to ありがとう to let the listener know that they're being thanked for the FAVOR. In more casual speech, the を can be completely dropped.


Word おめでとう(ございます)omedetou (gozaimasu)
Meaning congratulations; best wishes
Usage An expression used when congratulating someone for something or wishing them well.
おめでとう = casual
おめでとうごいざいます = polite

おめでとう(ございます)omedetou gozaimasu is an expression used for congratulating someone or wishing them well. Just like with ありがとう、おめでとうございます is more polite and おめでとう is more casual.

I mentioned this earlier, but a common trend you will see in Japanese is that longer phrases/words are more polite and shorter ones are more casual. This is seen in English as well, with abbreviated forms of phrases and words, like "congratulations" and "congrats."

がくせいgakusei
テストにごうかくした！tesuto ni goukaku shita!
カンたんkan-tan
おめでとうございます！omedetou gozaimasu!
Student
I passed the test!
Kan-tan
Congratulations!

Just like with ありがとう you can include words before おめでとう to be more specific. Like ごうかくおめでとうgoukaku omedetou (Congrats on passing). This is commonly done with おたんじょうびotanjoubi (birthday) to wish someone a happy birthday.

カンたんのおとうさんkan-tan no otou-san
きょうは11がつ20か、だよね？kyou wa juuichi gatsu hatsuka, da yo ne?
カンたんkan-tan
はい、そうです！hai sou desu!
カンたんのおとうさんkan-tan no otou-san
じゃあ、たんじょうびおめでとう！jaa, tanjoubi omedetou!
カンたんkan-tan
ありがとうございます！ケーキは？arigatou gozaimasu! keeki wa?
カンたんのおとうさんkan-tan no otou-san
そ、それは…sore wa...
Kan-tan's Father
Today is November 20th, isn't it?
Kan-tan
Yes, it is!
Kan-tan's Father
Happy Birthday, then!
Kan-tan
Thank you! Did you get me a cake?
Kan-tan's Father
W-Well, about that...

In casual speech, the おo in おたんじょうび can be dropped, like in the example above. This is an honorific prefix for nouns which makes them more polite.

Affirmation and Welcoming Words

Word はいhai
Meaning yes; understood
Usage A polite word for saying "yes."
It can also be used to say "OK" or "I understand."

はいhai is a word primarily used to say "yes" or to convey affirmation.

りんじんrinjin
カンたんはねこがとてもすきですね。kan-tan wa neko ga totemo suki desu ne.
カンたんkan-tan
はい！だいすきです！hai! daisuki desu!
Neighbor
You really like cats, don't you, Kan-tan?
Kan-tan
Yes! I love them!
 Please be careful not to say はい consecutively in response to someone (e.g., "はいはい") as it can come off rude or dismissive depending on the context and your tone. This carries the same feeling as saying "Yeah, yeah..." or "I know, I heard you the first time." in English. One はい is more than enough when responding to someone...unless, of course, you intend to be rude!

はい can also be used to say "OK" or to convey comprehension.

せんせいsensei
クラスはいじょうだ。kurasu wa ijou da.
せんせいsensei
カンたん、ごうれいをたのむ。kan-tan, gourei wo tanomu.
カンたんkan-tan
はい！きりつ…れい！hai! kiritsu...rei!
Teacher
That concludes today's class.
Teacher
Kan-tan, please lead the class.
Kan-tan
Understood! Stand...and bow!
In most Japanese schools, it is common for students to stand and bow at the beginning and end of class. The teacher will often designate a student to lead the class in this ritual. The primary purpose of this is to signal that class is either starting or ending and to keep the classroom coordinated and disciplined.

Word いいえiie
Meaning no; don't mention it
Usage A polite word for saying "no." It can also be used as a response for when someone thanks you for something you did.

いいえiie is a word primarily used to say "no."

ともだちtomodachi
カンたんはキュウリがすき？kan-tan wa kyuuri ga suki?
カンたんkan-tan
いいえ…だいきらいです！iie...daikirai desu!
Friend
Do you like cucumbers, Kan-tan?
Kan-tan
No...I hate them!

いいえ can also be used as a modest way to say "you're welcome." It's akin to saying "don't mention it" which holds the meaning of "there's no need to thank me (for something so trivial/something one would naturally do)."

ともだちtomodachi
いっしょにべんきょうしてくれてありがとう！issho ni benkyou shite kurete arigatou!
カンたんkan-tan
いいえ！たのしかったです！iie! tanoshikatta desu!
Friend
Thank you for studying with me!
Kan-tan
Don't mention it! I had fun!

Unlike はい, you can say いいえ consecutively (e.g., "いいえ、いいえ") in response to being thanked. There is actually a set phrase, いえいえieie, where the initial い is shortened. This is like saying "No, no!" or "No, not at all!" and is a response one can use to remain modest when being thanked.


Word どういたしましてdouitashimashite
Meaning you're welcome; don't mention it
Usage A polite way to say "you're welcome" to someone who thanked you.

どういたしましてdouitashimashite is a word primarily used to say "you're welcome." You'll often see it used in response to ありがとう.

がくせいgakusei
きのう、キャンディをありがとうございます！kinou, kyandi wo arigatou gozaimasu!
カンたんkan-tan
どういたしまして！douitashimashite!
Student
Thank you for the candy yesterday!
Kan-tan
You're welcome!

Please Words

Word おねがい(します)onegai (shimasu)
Meaning Please (do for me)
Usage A very polite expression used to ask a favor of another person.

おねがいしますonegai shimasu is a polite expression used for asking favors. It is commonly used when ordering at a restaurant, when asking someone to do something, etc.

レストランでresutoran de
カンたんkan-tan
アイスクリームをふたつおねがいします！aisu kuriimu wo futatsu onegai shimasu!
ウェイターweitaa
はい、アイスクリームをふたつですね。hai, aisu kuriimu wo futatsu desu ne.
カンたんkan-tan
はい！チョコミントとバニラです！hai! choco minto to banira desu!
ウェイターweitaa
しょうしょうおまちください。shoushou omachi kudasai.
At a restaurant
Kan-tan
I would like two ice creams, please!
Waiter
OK. Two ice creams, correct?
Kan-tan
Yes! Mint chocolate chip and vanilla!
Waiter
Please wait a moment (while we prepare your order).
を is optional when using おねがいします. Including を raises the level of formality, while excluding を lowers it.

When simply asking someone for a favor, you can just say "おねがいします！" after your request. Typically, one will put both of their hands together like they're praying and lower their head when saying this. Doing this gesture isn't necessary when ordering at a restaurant, however, and would actually look quite awkward, so only do this gesture for serious requests towards individuals.


Word どうぞdouzo
Meaning please; here you are; feel free
Usage Used when handing someone something, such as a present.
Used when giving permission, such as entering a room.

どうぞdouzo is a word with many different applications in the Japanese language, but some of its primary uses are giving permission, offering items, and so on.

ともだちtomodachi
とんとん――カンたんいるの～？tonton――kan-tan iru nooo?
カンたんkan-tan
は～い！haaai!
カンたんkan-tan
がちゃ――どうぞはいってください！gacha――douzo haitte kudasai!
Friend
*knock knock* Are you thereee, Kan-tan?
Kan-tan
I aaam!
Kan-tan
*opens door* Please come in!

In regards to gifts and other offerings, you can say どうぞ while holding out the item to a person.

カンたんのおとうさんkan-tan no otou-san
どうぞ、プレゼントだよ。douzo, purezento da yo.
カンたんkan-tan
わ～い！ほしかったキャンディです！waai! hoshikatta kyandi desu!
カンたんkan-tan
おとうさん、ありがとうございます！otou-san arigatou gozaimasu
Kan-tan's Father
Here, I got you a present.
Kan-tan
Yaaay! It's the candy I wanted!
Kan-tan
Thank you so much, Dad!

Apology Words

Word すみませんsumimasen
Meaning excuse me; I'm sorry
Usage A polite expression used when apologizing, excusing oneself, or grabbing attention.
Also used to say "thank you" when someone has helped you.

すみませんsumimasen is a polite expression with many different uses, but one of its primary uses is for apologizing.

カンたんはあるきながらおとこのひとにぶつかりました。kan-tan wa aruki nagara otoko no hito ni butsukari mashita.
カンたんkan-tan
あっ、すみません！a, sumimasen!
おとこのひとotoko no hito
いいえ。iie.
Kan-tan bumps into a man while walking.
Kan-tan
Ah, I'm sorry!
Man
Don't worry about it.

Aside from apologizing, すみません can also be used when you want to say "excuse me." For example, if you were trying to grab a waiter's attention, you can say すみません！ (excuse me!) to get their attention.

レストランで
カンたんkan-tan
すみませ～ん！sumimaseeen!
ウェイターweitaa
はい。ごちゅうもんはなににしますか？hai. gochuumon wa nani ni shimasu ka?
At a restauranrt
Kan-tan
Excuse meee!
Waiter
Yes? What can I get for you?

Another use of すみません is saying "thank you" to someone who has helped you.

きんじょのだがしやで、カンたんはさいふをおとしました。kinjo no dagashi-ya de, kan-tan wa saifu wo otoshi mashita.
おんなのひとonna no hito
あのう、すみません。anou, sumimasen.
おんなのひとonna no hito
このさいふはあなたのですか？kono saifu wa anata no desu ka?
カンたんkan-tan
あっ、はい！a, hai!

Dagashi-ya are small independentlty run stores that sell penny candy and other cheap snacks. Its name is formed from the word だがしdagashi (cheap candy) and the suffix や (store) giving it the literal meaning of "cheap candy store." Dagashi-ya have declined in recent years due to convience stores also selling cheap penny candy, so if you see one during your visit to Japan be sure to support them by buying lots of candy and snacks like Kan-tan does!

Word ごめん(なさい)gomen (nasai)
Meaning I'm sorry
Usage An expression used when apologizing.
ごめんなさい = standard, more polite
ごめん = more casual

ごめんなさいgomen nasai is a standard expression used for apologizing. In casual situations, the なさい is dropped and ごめん simply said, as seen in the example below.
