If you're reading this, then that means you must've learned all 46 Katakana characters, so congratulations! Now, just like with Hiragana, the next step is to learn about the Dakuten for Katakana. Overall, it is basically the same as the Hiragana Dakuten (I recommend reading the intro for this article if you need a refresher on Dakuten), so if you got the Dakuten down, you don't really need to spend too much time on this section and can even skip it if you like. Of course, you can still do everything here if you really want to pound the Dakuten into your memory.

Now then, to give you a quick refresher on what each of the marks mean: Dakuten resembles two dots or small lines and looks just like the double quotation mark: ". Handakuten resembles a small circle and is mainly used to change "h" to "p." Both of these marks are placed in the top right of each character and are also known as "diacritical marks," which are basically just small marks applied to characters to change their pronunciations.

There are a total of 25 additional sounds that we can create with Dakuten and Handakuten, and they're exactly the same as their Hiragana counterparts pronunciation-wise. If you're well-acquainted with the Hiragana Dakuten, going through this section should be a breeze. Let's begin!

ガga ギgi グgu ゲge ゴgo

First up is the ガga column which is just the カka column, but instead of "k" the first sound changes to a "g" for each character.


Katakana ガ
Romaji ga
Pronunciation が/gah

Katakana ギ
Romaji gi
Pronunciation ぎ/gee

Katakana グ
Romaji gu
Pronunciation ぐ/goo

Katakana ゲ
Romaji ge
Pronunciation げ/geh

Katakana ゴ
Romaji go
Pronunciation ご/goh

Additional Dakuten

Before we finish up this section, there's just one more character left to cover; ウu. When a dakuten is added to it, the sound changes to ヴvu which sounds fairly similar to ブbu. Historically, "v" sounds in Japanese were represented by the バba column, but in the 19th century ヴvu was added to the language to help cover words and names that had a "v" sound.

Katakana ヴ
Romaji vu
Pronunciation voo/boo
