In this section we will learn about numbers and counting in Japanese. If you're already familiar with your 1, 2, 3s, this section should be fairly easy since you're essentially just linking new pronunciations to numbers that you already know!

The Basic Numbers

To begin, let's take a look at how numbers 0-10 plus 100 are pronounced in Japanese.

Numeral Hiragana Audio
0 ゼロzero/れいrei rei
1 いちichi ichi
2 にni ni
3 さんsan san
4 よんyon/しshi yon
5 ごgo go
6 ろくroku roku
7 ななnana/しちshichi nana
8 はちhachi hachi
9 きゅうkyuu/くku kyuu
10 じゅうじゅう juu
100 ひゃくhyaku hyaku

As you can see above, some numbers have multiple pronunciations. The pronunciation used depends on a number of factors, such as the counter used with the number and even personal preference when counting. The most common pronunciation used in counting has been placed first, so just focus on memorizing these for now until you're ready to tie another pronunciation to the same numeral.

Counting

Counting works exactly the same as it does in English with a few differences here and there for Japanese. The best way to get a grasp of it is by actually counting, of course! So, let's begin with numbers 0-10 and we'll go over any exceptions along the way.

Numbers 0-10

ゼロ0
Zero's native pronunciation is れいrei which comes from the kanji for zero, 零れい. This kanji form is used more in formal contexts, however, and is replaced by the loanword ゼロzero in casual and conversational Japanese, and even counting!

いち1

に2

さん3

よん／し4
When counting from 1-10, 4 can be pronounced as よん or し. し is sometimes avoided for superstitious reasons because it has the same pronunciation as 死し (death). This is the reason why 4 is considered an "unlucky number" in Japan.

ご5

ろく6

なな／しち7
When counting from 1-10, 7 can be pronounced as なな or しち. Much like with し4, しち is sometimes avoided because it contains 死し (death) in the pronunciation. Unlike 4, however, 7 is considered a lucky number.

はち8

きゅう/く9
When counting from 1-10, 9 can be pronounced as きゅう or く. く is sometimes avoided because it sounds similar to 苦く (pain/suffering). Because of this, 9 is considered an unlucky number.

じゅう10

Not so hard, right? Try counting from 0-10 a few more times, then take a break for 5 or 10 minutes. After your break, see if you can count from 0 to 10 in Japanese in your head without looking at this page. If you can do it, congrats! If not, don't worry about it, since we'll be going over the numbers again in the next section below!

Numbers 11-100

In this section we'll be counting numbers 11-20, each tens unit after 20 and then 100. The good thing about this section is that all numbers use a single pronunciation. So when you see 4, 7, and 9 you only need to utilize their よん, なな, and きゅう readings! Furthermore, after じゅう10, the pronunciations for numbers repeat, meaning we take numbers 1-9 and place them after or before じゅう to make newer and bigger numbers. Let's try it out now!

じゅういち11

じゅうに12

じゅうさん13

じゅうよん14

じゅうご15

じゅうろく16

じゅうなな17

じゅうはち18

じゅうきゅう19

As you can see above, we simply put numbers 1-9 onto the end of じゅう10 to make 11, 12, 13, and so on. This can also be done by placing the numbers in front of じゅう10 to make 20, 30, 40, and so on. If you place numbers onto the front AND end of じゅう10 you can make にじゅういち21, にじゅうに22, にじゅうさん23, and so on.

For this next part we'll just cover the tens digits and encourage you to fill in the blanks as a challenge! The pronunciations for the ones digits are the same as 11-19 for にじゅう20, さんじゅう30, etc., so if you get stuck try referencing those.

にじゅう20

Try counting to にじゅうきゅう29!

さんじゅう30

Try counting to さんじゅうきゅう39!

よんじゅう40

Try counting to よんじゅうきゅう49!

ごじゅう50

Try counting to ごじゅうきゅう59!

ろくじゅう60

Try counting to ろくじゅうきゅう69! (ナイスnaisu)

ななじゅう70

Try counting to ななじゅうきゅう79!

はちじゅう80

Try counting to はちじゅうきゅう89!

きゅうじゅう90

Try counting to きゅうじゅうきゅう99!

ひゃく100

Great work on counting to ひゃく100! From here on you're free to count ever higher, such as to ひゃくじゅうご115, にひゃくろくじゅうなな267, and さんびゃく300-- ちょっとまってchotto matteWait a sec, did you notice that? ひゃくhyaku changed to びゃくbyaku when さん was put in front of it! This is just one of three exceptions with ひゃく100, and I'll explain these sound changes below.

300 さんびゃくsan byaku (the h in hyaku changes to b) NOT さんひゃくsan hyaku
600 ろっぴゃくroppyaku (the h in hyaku changes to pp, く is dropped on ろく) NOT ろくひゃくroku hyaku
800 はっぴゃくhappyaku (the h in hyaku changes to pp, ち is dropped on はち) NOT はちひゃくhachi hyaku

All you need to do is memorize these three exceptions for ひゃく and you'll have no problems when counting in the hundreds!

Closing

That's it for numbers in this lesson! Next time we'll cover the thousands, ten thousands, and more. But for now, you should focus on memorizing these numbers, and the best way to do that is by practicing! Here's a few suggestions on doing that:

  • Trying counting in your head using the numbers you learned or counting things you see in real life, but in Japanese!
  • Challenge yourself by doing the practice exercises under the numbers section!
  • Review the numbers for this lesson, here, if you need a refresher.

Once you have a good grasp of counting in Japanese, the last and final step for this lesson...is Kanji!
