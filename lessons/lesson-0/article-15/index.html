The Basic Numbers

To begin, let's take a look at how numbers 0-10 plus 100 are pronounced in Japanese.

Numeral Hiragana Audio
0 ゼロzero/れいrei rei
1 いちichi ichi
2 にni ni
3 さんsan san
4 よんyon/しshi yon
5 ごgo go
6 ろくroku roku
7 ななnana/しちshichi nana
8 はちhachi hachi
9 きゅうkyuu/くku kyuu
10 じゅうじゅう juu
100 ひゃくhyaku hyaku

As you can see above, some numbers have multiple pronunciations. The pronunciation used depends on a number of factors, such as the counter used with the number and even personal preference when counting. The most common pronunciation used in counting has been placed first, so just focus on memorizing these for now until you're ready to tie another pronunciation to the same numeral.

Counting

Counting works exactly the same as it does in English with a few differences here and there for Japanese. The best way to get a grasp of it is by actually counting, of course! So, let's begin with numbers 0-10 and we'll go over any exceptions along the way.

Numbers 0-10

ゼロ0

いち1

に2

さん3

よん／し4
When counting from 1-10, 4 can be pronounced as よん or し. し is sometimes avoided for superstition reasons because it has the same pronunciation as 死し (death). This is the reason why 4 is considered an "unlucky number" in Japan.

ご5

ろく6

なな／しち7
When counting from 1-10, 7 can be pronounced as なな or しち. Much like with し4, しち is sometimes avoided because it contains 死し (death) in the pronunciation. Unlike 4, however, 7 is considered a lucky number.

はち8

きゅう/く9
When counting from 1-10, 9 can be pronounced as きゅう or く. く is sometimes avoided because it sounds similar to 苦く (pain/suffering). Because of this, 9 is considered an unlucky number.

じゅう10

Not so hard, right? Try counting from 0-10 a few more times, then take a break for 5 or 10 minutes. After your break, see if you can count from 0 to 10 in Japanese in your head without looking at this page. If you can do it, congrats! If not, don't worry about it, since we'll be going over the numbers again in the next section below!

Numbers 11-100
