In this article we'll learn about Katakana's Digraphscombo kana and just like with the Dakuten, all of the combinations are basically the same as the ones we learned in the Hiragana Digraphs article (read the intro for this article if you need a refresher on what Digraphs are) which should make going through this section a breeze. There's only a few new combinations in the シャ, ジャ, and チャ columns and we'll try to cover their pronunciations like we did with the Hiragana articles.

When you're ready, head on over to the first section below to begin learning about Katakana's Digraphs!

シャsha シュshu シェshe ショsho

Next up is the シャsha column which takes the "sh" sound from シshi and combines it with ャya, ュyu, and ョyo. However, the difference with this column is that the "y" sound is NOT pronounced here. We're essentially taking "sh" from シ and "a/u/o" from "ヤ/ユ/ヨ" to create our sounds.

There's also a new combination in this column; シェshe, which combines シshi with a small ェe.


Katakana シャ
Romaji sha
Pronunciation しゃ/shah

Katakana シュ
Romaji shu
Pronunciation しゅ/shoo

Katakana シェ
Romaji she
Pronunciation sheh

シェshe is pronounced like a combination of シshi (minus the "i") and ェe. The sound you create should be similar to sheh ("sh" sound followed by "eh"). It's also pronounced like the she in "shed" or "shell."

My neighbor's シェshed is jam-packed full of シェshells that he collected from the beach.

Katakana ショ
Romaji sho
Pronunciation しょ/shoh
