If you're reading this, then that means you must've learned all 46 Hiragana characters, so おめでとうomedetou (congrats) on making it this far! But wait, did you notice something funny on the てte in the Japanese word that I just used? This is called a Dakuten and changes the sound that the character makes, so instead of te the sound became de.

Dakuten resembles two dots or small lines and looks just like the double quotation mark: ". Handakuten resembles a small circle and is mainly used to change "h" to "p." Both of these marks are placed in the top right of each character and are also known as "diacritical marks," which are basically just small marks applied to characters to change their pronunciations.

There are a total of 25 additional sounds that we can create with Dakuten and Handakuten, and we're going to go over each one below to help you memorize them and master their pronunciations. This should actually be a lot easier than earlier, since all we're doing is shifting the first sound for characters that we already know. Now, let's get started!

  1. が ぎ ぐ げ ご
  2. ざ じ ず ぜ ぞ
  3. だ ぢ づ で ど
  4. ば び ぶ べ ぼ
  5. ぱ ぴ ぷ ぺ ぽ

がga ぎgi ぐgu げge ごgo

First up is the がga column which is just the かka column, but instead of "k" the first sound changes to a "g" for each character.


Hiragana が
Romaji ga
Pronunciation gah

がga is pronounced like the ga in "galactic." It's also pronounced exactly like the interjection "gah!"

がGah! That がGalactic baseballer's swing is no joke!

Hiragana ぎ
Romaji gi
Pronunciation gee

ぎgi is pronounced like the gee in "geese" or "geek."

I'm a real ぎgeek when it comes to ぎgeese watching.

Hiragana ぐ
Romaji gu
Pronunciation goo

ぐgu is pronounced like the goo in "goofy", "gooey", and "goon."

I was being chased by this ぐgoofy looking ぐgoon until he slipped on something ぐgooey on the floor.

Hiragana げ
Romaji ge
Pronunciation geh

げge is pronounced like the ge in "gecko" and "get."

I asked my mother to げget me a plush of that げgecko that's always on TV.

Hiragana ご
Romaji go
Pronunciation goh

ごgo is pronounced just like "go" or like the go in "goat."

There was a wild ごgoat ごgoing around and terrorizing our small town.

ざza じji ずzu ぜze ぞzo

Next up is the ざza column which is just the さsa column, but instead of "s" the first sound changes to a "z" for each character, except for じji. For the most part, we're basically just flipping the "S" to make a "Z!"


Hiragana ざ
Romaji za
Pronunciation zah

ざza is pronounced like the za in "plaza" or like the za in the Japanese word for mob/ganster, "yakuza."

The yakuざza were chasing after me for horse drawings that I made, but I fortunately managed to shake them at the city plaざza.

Hiragana じ
Romaji ji
Pronunciation jee

じji is an exception to the "z" sound in this column and is instead pronounced like the jee in "jeez" or "jeep."

じJeez, would you get in the じjeep already? The やくざyakuza are still hot on our trail!

You may see じji romanized as zi where the Kunrei system is still used. You can also type zi in addition to ji to write じ.


Hiragana ず
Romaji zu
Pronunciation zoo

ずzu is pronounced just like "zoo" or like the zoo in "zoom."

When I was at the ずzoo, an escaped zebra ずzoomed right past me.

Hiragana ぜ
Romaji ze
Pronunciation zeh

ぜze is pronounced like the ze in "zest" and "zeppelin."

Why am I ぜzesting a lemon while riding on a ぜzeppelin you ask? Not even I know; the example sentence made me do it.

Hiragana ぞ
Romaji zo
Pronunciation zoh

ぞzo is pronounced like the zo in "zone" or "zodiac."

Each responder was assigned to a different ぞzone of the city based on their ぞzodiac to help with cleanup efforts. Their superstitious boss said that it helped improve efficiency, but they just think he's crazy.

だda ぢdi づdu でde どdo

Next up is the だda column which is just the たta column, but instead of "t" the first sound changes to a "d" for each character.


Hiragana だ
Romaji da
Pronunciation dah

だda is pronounced similarly to the da in "panda" or "soda," but with a little more emphasis on the da making it a dah sound like in "dahlia."

だDahlia the panだda had a peculiar habit of only drinking soだda. Whenever given water, she'd turn her nose up at it.

Hiragana ぢ
Romaji di
Pronunciation jee/じ

ぢdi is pronounced exactly like じji, or if you need a refresher, like the jee in "jeez" and "jeep."

ぢJeez! Those darn やくざyakuza beat up my poor ぢjeep...

In the past it was pronounced more like dzi which is like combining a d sound with a zee sound.


Hiragana づ
Romaji du
Pronunciation zoo/ず

づdu is pronounced exactly like ずzu, or if you need a refresher, like "zoo" or like the zoo in "zoom."

If you づzoom in on this picture here, you can see the local づzoo.

In the past it was pronounced more like dzu which is like combining a d sound with a zoo sound.


Hiragana で
Romaji de
Pronunciation deh

でde is pronounced like the de in "den" or "dead."

Watch out! There's a でdeadly rabbit in the でden ahead.

Hiragana ど
Romaji do
Pronunciation doh

どdo is pronounced just like Homer Simpson's famous "d'oh!" It's also pronounced like the do in "donut."

どD'oh! I forgot my box of どdonuts at home!

ばba びbi ぶbu べbe ぼbo

Next up is the ばba column which is just the はha column, but instead of "h" the first sound changes to a "b" for each character.


Hiragana ば
Romaji ba
Pronunciation bah

ばba is pronounced like the ba in "roomba" and "scuba."

I found a roomばba while scuばba diving. Was someone trying to clean the sea floor with it?

Hiragana び
Romaji bi
Pronunciation bee

びbi is pronounced just like "bee" or like the bee in "beef."

A びbee stung me while I was cooking some びbeef on the grill.

Hiragana ぶ
Romaji bu
Pronunciation boo

ぶbu is pronounced just like "boo!" It's also pronounced like the boo in "boot."

While I was taking a walk at night, someone behind me yelled "ぶBOO!" and scared me right out of my ぶboots!

Hiragana べ
Romaji be
Pronunciation beh

べbe is pronounced like the be in "bed" and "bench."

べBen was a peculiar fellow who liked to use a べbench as a べbed.

Hiragana ぼ
Romaji bo
Pronunciation boh

ぼbo is pronounced like the bo in "boat" and "bone."

To escape the island, the man made a ぼboat out of the ぼbones he found scattered about.

ぱpa ぴpi ぷpu ぺpe ぽpo

Last but not least is the ぱpa column which is just the はha column, but instead of "h" the first sound changes to a "p" for each character.


Hiragana ぱ
Romaji pa
Pronunciation pah

ぱpa is pronounced like the pa in "grandpa" and "spa."

My grandぱpa used to love going to the sぱpa.

Hiragana ぴ
Romaji pi
Pronunciation pee

ぴpi is pronounced just like "pee" or like the pee in "peep."

You should NEVER ぴpeep on another person while they're ぴpeeing!

Hiragana ぷ
Romaji pu
Pronunciation poo

ぷpu is pronounced just like the pooh in "Winnie-the-pooh!" It's also pronounced like the poo in "shampoo."

Do you think Winnie-the-ぷpooh uses shamぷpoo? If so, what would his favorite fragrance be?

Hiragana ぺ
Romaji pe
Pronunciation peh

ぺpe is pronounced like the pe in "pepper" and "pet."

My ぺpet dog really loves green ぺpeppers.

Hiragana ぽ
Romaji po
Pronunciation poh

ぽpo is pronounced like the po in "poke" and "pony."

A man kept ぽpoking a ぽpony until it kicked him. Don't ぽpoke the ぽpony!
