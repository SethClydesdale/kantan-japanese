In this article we'll learn about Katakana's Extended Digraphs. These digraphs use small vowels, such as ァa, ィi, ゥu, ェe and ォo, and the small ュyu. They were added to support the pronunciation of foreign words and names that didn't previously exist in the language. While you may not ecounter these digraphs as much, they're still worth learning for if you DO run into them.

Now this is all new territory, so we'll be covering the pronunciation of these characters this time to help you get the hang of them. When you're ready, head on over to the first section below to begin learning about Katakana's Extended Digraphs!

ウィwi ウェwe ウォwo

First up is the ウィwi column which combines ウu with a small ィi, ェe, and ォo to create a "w" sound.


Katakana ウィ
Romaji wi
Pronunciation wee

ウィwi is pronounced like the wee in "week" or "weed."

Next ウィweek I have to pull ウィweeds at my Mom's house.

Katakana ウェ
Romaji we
Pronunciation weh

ウェwe is pronounced like the we in "web" or "well."

The ウェwell out back was full of spider ウェwebs from not being used.

Katakana ウォ
Romaji wo (who)
Pronunciation woah

ウォwo is pronounced like the wo in "woah" or "woke."

I ウォwoke up screaming "ウォwoah!" because I had a dream where I almost fell off a skyscraper.

When writing this digraph with romaji input you must type who (then press Space if using Hiragana input) to write ウォ.

ヴァva ヴィvi ヴvu ヴェve ヴォvo

Next up is the ヴァva column which combines ヴvu with small vowels to create "v" sounds. Most Japanese can't really tell the difference between "v" or "b" so they end up pronouncing a lot of these characters like they would the バba column.


Katakana ヴァ
Romaji va
Pronunciation vah

ヴァva is pronounced like the va in "vannila" or "Vanessa."

ヴァvanessa likes simple flavors and always prefers to have ヴァvannila cake for dessert.

Katakana ヴィ
Romaji vi
Pronunciation vee

ヴィvi is pronounced just like the letter "V" or like the vi in "viva"

It should be obvious, but ヴィviva begins with the letter ヴィV.

Katakana ヴ
Romaji vu
Pronunciation voo

ヴvu is pronounced like like the voo in "voodoo."

Ow! I've been getting pains all over today. Does someone have a ヴvoodoo doll of me or something?

Katakana ヴェ
Romaji ve
Pronunciation veh

ヴェve is pronounced like the ve in "very" or "vegetable."

My son likes ヴェvegetables ヴェvery much unlike my friend's son who feeds them to their dog.

Katakana ヴォ
Romaji vo
Pronunciation voh

ヴォvo is pronounced like the vo in "vocabulary" or "vocal."

ヴォVocabulary is always easier to remember when you ヴォvocalize the words while visualizing what the word means in your mind.

クァkwa クィkwi クェkwe クォkwo

Next up is the クァkwa column which combines クku with small vowels to create "kw" sounds. In English, these digraphs sound very similar to the English "Q", so you can expect a lot of our examples for pronunciation to use Q words.


Katakana クァ
Romaji kwa (qa)
Pronunciation kwah

クァkwa is pronounced like the qua in "quality" or "quadruple."

The クァquality of the project クァquadrupled when it received more funding and support.

In addition to kwa you can also write クァ by typing qa with romaji input.


Katakana クィ
Romaji kwi (qi)
Pronunciation kwee

クィkwi is pronounced like the quee in "queen" or the que in "query."

The クィqueen responded to my クィquery about her recent absence.

クィ can by written by typing qi with romaji input.


Katakana クェ
Romaji kwe (qe)
Pronunciation kweh

クェkwe is pronounced like the qua in "quake" or "quaint."

There was an earthクェquake in our クェquaint little village the other day.

クェ can by written by typing qe with romaji input.


Katakana クォ
Romaji kwo (qo)
Pronunciation kwoh

クォkwo is pronounced like the quo in "quote" or "quota."

I stayed up all night working, but I still haven't compiled enough クォquotes to meet my クォquota...

クォ can by written by typing qo with romaji input.

ツァtsa ツィtsi ツェtse ツォtso

Next up is the ツァtsa column which combines ツtsu with small vowels to create a "ts" sound. These digraphs are basically meant to represent "Z" sounds, like the za in Mozart, for example.


Katakana ツァ
Romaji tsa
Pronunciation tsah

ツァtsa is pronounced like the za in "Mozart" or "Mozzarella."

The secret to making my Moツァzzarella cheese taste so good is that I let it listen to Moツァzart while it ages.

Katakana ツィ
Romaji tsi
Pronunciation tsee

ツィtsi is pronounced like like the zi in "paparazzi."

Kan-tan became so popular that she could no longer go to the local dagashiya anymore, because the paparaツィzzi would get in the way of her buying candy.

Katakana ツェ
Romaji tse
Pronunciation tseh

ツェtse is pronounced like the ze in "pretzel" and "pizzeria."

The piツェzzeria that I go to has a new pretツェzel crust that is to die for!

Katakana ツォ
Romaji tso
Pronunciation tsoh

ツォtso is pronounced like the zo in "calzone," with a little more of a ts sound so that it doesn't sound like "zone" but "tsone."

All of these Italian words got me craving a calツォzone.

ティti テュtyu トゥtu

Next up is the ティti column which combines テte with a small ィ and ュ, and トto with a small ゥ.


Katakana ティ
Romaji ti (thi)
Pronunciation tee

ティti is pronounced just like "tea" or like the ty in "party." This digraph is used for a lot of words that contain the "ti" sound or end in "ty."

ティTio attended a ティtea parティty with her good friend Renne.

ティ can be written by typing thi with romaji input.


Katakana テュ
Romaji tyu (thu)
Pronunciation tyoo

テュtyu is pronounced like the tu in "tutor," but the beginning has more of a "y" sound like t-you-tor. This digraph is used for some words that have a "tu" sound, though it is fairly rare due to being difficult to pronounce for natives. You'll often see チュchu used in its place to represent this sound.

My テュtutor tried to teach me about テュtyu, but I said I'll just use チュchu instead.

テュ can be written by typing thu with romaji input.


Katakana トゥ
Romaji tu (twu)
Pronunciation too

トゥtu is pronounced just like two or like the too in "tooth." Unlike テュtyu, トゥtu is more common when used to represent "tu" sounds in foreign words.

When I put a トゥtooth under my pillow as a kid, I'd wake up to find トゥtwo dollars in its place.

トゥ can be written by typing twu with romaji input.

ディdi デュdyu ドゥdu

Next up is the ディdi column which combines デde with a small ィ and ュ, and ドdo with a small ゥ.


Katakana ディ
Romaji di (dhi)
Pronunciation dee

ディdi is pronounced just like the letter "D" or like the dy in "candy." This digraph is used for a lot of words that contain the "di" sound or end in "dy."

The word "canディdy" has ONE letter ディD in it. (I can count unlike AI)

ディ can be written by typing dhi with romaji input.


Katakana デュ
Romaji dyu (dhu)
Pronunciation dyoo

デュdyu is pronounced like the du in "duet" and "duke" but the "du" is pronounced more with a "y" sound like d-you-et and d-you-ke (pronounce it all combined once you get the flow down). Unlike テュtyu, デュdyu is used for many more loan words and is typically used for words that contain a "du" sound but leaning more towards dyu (or dju) in certain dialects and languages.

The デュduke performed a デュduet with his wife.

デュ can be written by typing dhu with romaji input.


Katakana ドゥ
Romaji du (dwu)
Pronunciation doo

ドゥdu is pronounced like the doo in "doom" or like the du in "medusa." You might ask, why do we need two different digraphs to express "du"? The key is in their differences, where デュdyu represents a "du" with a "y" sound and ドゥdu closer representing the standard "du" which is pronounced similar to "do" or "dew." When it comes to frequency, however, デュdyu wins this time for representing "du" in loanwords.

I knew I was ドゥdoomed the moment I laid my eyes upon that vile meドゥdusa as my body slowly started turning to stone...

ドゥ can be written by typing dwu with romaji input.

ファfa フィfi フュfyu フェfe フォfo

Last up is the ファfa column which combines フfu with small vowels or the small ュ to create various "f" sounds not native to the language.


Katakana ファ
Romaji fa
Pronunciation fah

ファfa is pronounced like the fa in "fan" or "fast." It is usually used for words that contain "fa", but it's also used for ones that contain "fu" like "fuzzy" (ファジーfajii) or "fi" like "fight" (ファイトfaito). Because of this, it can be pronounced either with a sharp "a" like in "fax" or with a soft "a" like in "farm" depending on the word.

I'm a big ファfan of super ファfast cars.

Katakana フィ
Romaji fi
Pronunciation fee

フィfi is pronounced like the fi in "field" or "film." Just like with ファfa, フィfi has varied uses and pronunciations depending on the word. Besides "fi" it can also be used for "e" like in "feet" (フィートfiito) or "fever" (フィーバーfiibaa).

Expedition Team X traveled to a rural フィfield where a crop circle was to try and catch a UFO on フィfilm.

Katakana フュ
Romaji fyu
Pronunciation fyoo

フュfyu is pronounced just like "phew" or like the fu in "fuel" and "future." フュ is mainly used for foreign words that contain "fu" but with a "y" sound similar to "fuel" and "future" rather than the standard "fu" sound like in "fun" or "fudge."

I had a dream I was in the フュfuture and フュfuel prices where outrageous. フュPhew... good thing it was just a dream! ...or was it?

Katakana フェ
Romaji fe
Pronunciation feh

フェfe is pronounced like the fe in "festival" or "fence."

I couldn't afford to go to the fireworks フェfestival so I always used to watch it over the フェfence.

Katakana フォ
Romaji fo
Pronunciation foh

フォfo is pronounced like the fo in "folder" or "form."

When I opened my フォfolder my heart skipped a beat, because I had フォforgotten the フォform my teacher asked me to bring.
