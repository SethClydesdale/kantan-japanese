Kantan Japanese can be used for self-study, in a classroom setting, and more. But how exactly do you study with a website? If you're a student already taking a course, you can use our website to supplement your studies, such as searching for and doing exercises on the Lessons page related to grammar points you're having difficulty with or reviewing content from class that's available on our website.

If you're studying on your own, however, you can use all of the content on our website to learn Japanese from zero, just like a textbook! Below is our suggested approach to studying Japanese with our website. You can follow it completely or use it to get a general idea of how you'd like to tackle each lesson.

  1. Japanese Writing System
  2. Vocabulary Sections
  3. Grammar Sections
  4. Practice Sections
  5. Kanji Sections
  6. After Completing a Lesson

1. Japanese Writing System

If this is your first time learning Japanese, it is imperative that you memorize the writing system first. Lesson 0 on our website offers resources and exercises for learning both Hiragana and Katakana. It is recommended to start with Hiragana first, followed by Katakana once you have mastered Hiragana. Lesson 0 will also introduce you to Kanji which is the most important aspect of the Japanese language.

Because learning a new script for reading and writing can be very difficult, it is suggested that you take your time and don't rush. Furthermore, don't be discouraged if you forget a character or are very slow at reading. The more you forget and recall, and the more you see/utilize the characters that you learned, the easier it will become to read and write. A little practice each day, whether reading or writing, can go a long way!

Kantan Japanese will try to use as much Hiragana, Katakana, and Kanji as possible to help you with memorizing the Japanese writing system. And for Lessons 0-2 we will also include romaji where necessary to help those who may still be having trouble with memorizing the kana.

2. Vocabulary Sections

Each lesson has a vocabulary section which roughly contains 50-100 new words. Your goal for each lesson should be to memorize as many words as possible by:

  1. Reviewing the vocabulary chart.
  2. Doing the vocabulary exercises.
  3. Doing vocabulary reviews when available. (optional; SRS must be enabled)

To help reinforce memorization, Kantan Japanese tries to use as much of the current and past vocabulary learned in exercises, examples, reading material and more. You can also find tips for memorizing vocabulary here.

3. Grammar Sections

With the exception of Lesson 0, all lessons on Kantan Japanese will teach you around 5-10 new grammar points that you can utilize in conversation, reading, and so on. Grammar is a very important aspect of any language and slowly, but constantly, building upon your knowledge of it will bring you closer and closer to fluency. Your goal for the grammar section is simple:

  1. Read the explanations for each grammar point.
  2. Look at the examples to understand usage.
  3. Re-read any points you don't completely understand. (either immediately or after a day)

Of course, if a point is still unclear after re-reading, feel free to ask your teacher for help or ask for clarification on our Reddit.

4. Practice Sections

The practice section for each lesson consists of various exercises for practicing the content taught in the Grammar section. It aims to help solidify the grammar points you learned by putting them into action. So, your goal for this section is to:

  1. Do each practice exercise.
  2. Review grammar in the Grammar section. (if you need to refresh)
  3. Do any exercises you previously failed again. (either immediately or after a day)

For any exercies you may be having trouble with, it is recommended to go back and review the grammar points for that exercise to help you get a better idea of how they work. And of course, if you're still having trouble after that, don't be afraid to reach out for help!

5. Kanji Sections

The Kanji section will teach you around 10-25 kanji per lesson. Your goal for this section should be to:

  1. Review the kanji chart. Look at the character, the readings, meanings, and example words.
  2. Do the kanji exercises.
  3. Do kanji reviews when available. (optional; SRS must be enabled)

Kanji can be tricky to get the hang of, but don't give up! It's a very important aspect of the language that can help you easily understand the meaning of a word at a glance or give you a general idea of what a word you don't know means.

Our recommendation is to study Kanji alongside words, because it is a great way to reinforce their meanings and readings. Not to mention, the more you see the words you studied alongside that kanji, the easier it will become to remember what that kanji means. Ultimately, it comes down to repetition; seeing the same kanji over and over again in context really helps to cement it into your memory.

6. After Completing a Lesson

After completing a lesson, there's a few things you can do, such as:

  • Review words, grammar, or kanji you didn't fully comprehend.
  • Take a break from studying for a day or two. Rest can help fortify your memory and help you to feel refreshed.
  • Move onto the next lesson.

When you are ready for the next lesson, simply repeat steps 2-6.
